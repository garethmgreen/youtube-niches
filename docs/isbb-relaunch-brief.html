The call

Restart it. Don't resume it.

The demand is far bigger than it was when you quit, and your best tools are newer than your best video. But the format you were making — four-minute AI clips plus a wall of Shorts — is the single worst-performing shape in this niche now, and it was already failing while you were still uploading. Going back and posting more of the same would replay the exact decline in the table below.

Keep the channel, the name and the brand. Change the length, pick a lane, and fix the authenticity framing before a single new upload goes live.

Part four — The gate

Monetization is the thing that could stop all of this

On 15 July 2026 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" rule to "inauthentic content" in the Partner Program. It targets mass-produced, repetitive uploads that lack genuine originality — synthetic narration over interchangeable footage, every video looking and sounding the same. Thousands of faceless AI channels lost monetization under it, and in January 2026 YouTube terminated sixteen channels holding a combined 35 million subscribers.

You can see it in the table: of the fifteen AI-built channels surveyed, ten are not monetized. This is not a hypothetical risk in this niche — it is the base rate.

What the five monetized ones do differently

They disclose, and they add real editorial work. Cave Stories states its AI use directly in its channel About — "How I use AI: The voiceover uses standard, generic…" — and builds each video on a genuine researched incident. The AI is a production tool applied to real material, not the material itself.

Your current framing sits on the wrong side of this line. Your channel description says the stories are "rooted in real events but told through my own characters," and your video copy says "based on real events." Invented stories presented in the grammar of survivor testimony is precisely the pattern the policy is written to catch. You do not have to abandon fiction — you have to stop dressing it as documentary. Pick one and label it plainly.

What I'd actually do

Feature-length first-person survival testimony

Here is the gap nobody in this data is standing in. The AI-movie channels all make third-person genre films. The documentary channels all make third-person narration at 10–25 minutes. Not one of them is doing the thing that made I Shouldn't Be Alive work in the first place — the survivor's own voice, in the first person, at feature length.

That was your original instinct. Your channel name is already built on it, and your one genuine hit — "48 DAYS LOST: A Storm Capsized Our Boat At Sea" — is the video that used it most directly. Everyone else dropped the half of the format that carries the emotion, because third-person narration is easier to script. Doing the harder half is what makes you difficult to clone when the next wave of channels arrives.

It also resolves the policy problem cleanly: a first-person account of an invented character is a radio drama, and you label it as one — a survival story, told as fiction, at the length of a film. No claim of documentary, no ambiguity, no exposure.

Part seven — Before you upload anything

Channel fixes, in this order

These take an afternoon between them and every one of them is currently working against you.

  • DescriptionCut "rooted in real events" and "based on real events" from the channel About and from all nine video descriptions. Replace with a plain line: original fictional survival stories, produced with AI. This is the single highest-value edit on the list.
  • DisclosureAdd an explicit AI-use note to the About section, the way the monetized channels in this niche do. Tick the altered-content disclosure on every upload. Disclosure is what separates the five monetized AI channels from the ten that aren't.
  • ShortsLeave the 33 up — they do no harm — but stop making them as a primary output. Future Shorts should be cut from a finished long video, pointing at it.
  • TitlesRetitle the six failed videos into first person. They will not suddenly perform, but they make the channel read as one coherent product to anyone who lands on it from video one.
  • CaptionsYour hit video has no captions. Add them to everything — this niche has heavy silent-autoplay traffic and it costs you nothing.
  • BannerThe current banner sells an anthology of clips. It should sell one thing: feature-length survival stories, told by the person who lived them.
