## WHY THIS VIDEO, AND WHAT CHANGES FROM RED HAIR

The Red Hair video launched at **1.6% CTR and 0:51 average view duration**. Both
numbers point at the same two fixable problems, and this package fixes them
deliberately.

| Problem | Diagnosis | Fix in this video |
|---|---|---|
| **1.6% CTR** | Title promised something vague — "What Science Actually Shows" commits to nothing. Audience was also tiny (1–2% of people are redheads). | Title now promises a **specific contradiction** ("They're Not Actually Blue"). Audience is 8–10% globally, 70%+ in Scandinavia. |
| **0:51 AVD** | The opening asked a question but did not pay anything off inside the first minute. Viewers bailed before the MC1R explanation at 0:45. | The **payoff lands at 0:20**, not 0:45. The single best fact in the video is spent immediately, not saved. |

**The core lesson to carry forward:** do not save your best fact for the middle.
Spend it in the first 30 seconds and use the rest of the video to explain it.
Retention is bought at the front, not the back.

## KEY FACTS (load-bearing science)

**Solid — state these confidently:**

- There is no blue pigment in the human iris. Blue eyes are *structural colour*,
  produced by light scattering in the stroma of the iris — the same Rayleigh/Tyndall
  scattering that makes the sky appear blue.
- Brown eyes contain melanin; blue eyes are the near-absence of it in the front
  layer of the iris.
- The **HERC2** gene contains a regulatory variant that suppresses the neighbouring
  **OCA2** gene, reducing melanin production in the iris.
- Blue eyes are roughly **8–10% of the global population**.
- Frequency is extraordinarily high in the Baltic and Nordic countries — Estonia is
  commonly cited near 99%, with Finland, Sweden, Denmark, Norway and Iceland all
  very high.
- Ancient DNA from Mesolithic European remains (notably **La Braña 1** in Spain and
  the **Loschbour** individual in Luxembourg) shows the blue-eye variant present
  alongside genes for **dark skin** — meaning blue eyes predate pale European skin.
- Eye colour is polygenic. HERC2/OCA2 is the dominant factor, but it is not a
  simple one-gene switch, and the old "two blue-eyed parents can't have a
  brown-eyed child" rule is false.

**Contested — attribute, don't assert:**

- The claim that **all** blue-eyed people descend from a single common ancestor
  who lived 6,000–10,000 years ago comes from **Hans Eiberg's team at the
  University of Copenhagen (2008)**. It is widely reported and plausible, but it is
  one team's interpretation. Say *"researchers estimate"* or *"one Copenhagen team
  concluded"* — never *"we know"*.

**DO NOT:**

- Invent percentages, studies, or researcher names.
- Say "scientists were shocked" or any variant of it.
- Claim eye colour follows simple dominant/recessive inheritance. It doesn't.
- Imply blue eyes prove Viking descent. They indicate Northern European ancestry.
  Overclaiming is what gets ancestry channels flagged as pseudoscience.
- Tease a reveal that doesn't arrive.

## TONE

**Write like this:**
- Confident and declarative. The physics here is settled; do not hedge it.
- Second person throughout. *Your* eyes, *your* ancestry.
- Concrete. Name the gene, name the country, name the percentage.
- Give the viewer something to *do* — look in a mirror, look at a family member.

**Not like this:**
- No "some scientists believe" padding on facts that are established.
- No recapping. Never summarise what was just said; move forward.
- No filler transitions ("but that's not all", "here's where it gets interesting").
