The finding

Length is the lever, and the drop-off is a cliff rather than a slope.

Sorting all 116 reels by length shows something unusually clean. Everything under 30 seconds performs in a different league, and the fall between the 20–30s band and the 30–45s band is a factor of about eighteen — not a gentle decline.

The retention numbers explain the mechanism. Reels under 20 seconds hold 90% of viewers on average; reels over 60 seconds hold 37%. Facebook's recommendation engine reads that difference and decides whether to keep pushing a reel to people who don't follow you. Since 94% of your watch time comes from exactly that source, retention is not one metric among many — it is the whole distribution mechanism.

August

Two things went wrong, and only one of them is length.

July was your best month — a median of 14,660 views. August fell to 2,936. Part of that is straightforward: 13 of your 17 August reels were 45 seconds or longer, and you published exactly one short reel all month.

But length isn't the whole story. Holding length constant — looking only at reels 45 seconds and over — retention had been stable around 38–44% from May through July, then fell to 27% in August at the same median length. Your long reels didn't just stay long; they got materially less watchable.

That is Facebook doing what it is designed to do: it stopped showing your reels to strangers because the retention signal told it not to. It isn't a penalty, and there is nothing to appeal.

Where the views come from

Your 33,000 followers are almost irrelevant to your reach.

Across all 116 reels, watch time splits 94% recommendations, 5.9% followers, 0.1% shares. Your follower base contributes roughly 1,467 hours against 23,248 hours from people who have never heard of you.

This is worth internalising, because it reframes what a bad reel means. A reel that gets 2,000 views hasn't been rejected by your audience — it has been declined by the recommendation engine, and your followers were most of who saw it. The follower count is a floor, not an engine. Every breakout you have had came from strangers.

The two curves separate almost immediately and never reconverge. By a quarter of the way in, the gap is already most of what it will ever be — which tells you the opening seconds, not the payoff, are doing the sorting.

What to do

Four changes, in the order they'll pay.

  1. Cap the next twenty reels at 30 seconds

    Not "shorter" — capped. This is the highest-confidence finding in the data and it costs you nothing to test. If it works you'll know inside a week, because recommendation traffic arrives fast or not at all.

  2. Cut to the payoff, don't compress the whole story

    The 17-second reels that hit 466K and 450K retained over 99% — viewers watched them more than once. That comes from making one point cleanly, not from narrating a 70-second script at speed.

  3. Lead with a person, and with the correction

    Name the character in the first line and state what everyone gets wrong. Save the institutional and massacre material for when you have the reach to spend on it.

  4. Re-cut your existing winners

    Your 305K reel is 69 seconds and your 143K is 71. Both already proved the topic works. Cutting each into a 20-second version is the cheapest inventory you have.

What this can't tell you

The honest limits of this data.

  • The export starts 27 April, not 1 January. Whatever you posted before that isn't here, so "when did it change" only reaches back three and a half months.
  • 78 of 116 reels have no title. Topic conclusions come from the 38 that do, and those are almost all May–June — the weaker period. Both 450K+ reels are untitled, so what actually made them work is unknown.
  • This is observational, not an experiment. Short reels might also have been your better ideas, or your better edits. Length is the strongest single explanation in the data and it holds within every month, but the clean test is deliberate: post a mediocre idea short and a strong idea long, and compare.
  • August reels are young. Reels published in the last few days hadn't finished accumulating views. Restricting to reels at least ten days old, the August median is still about 3,464 — so the decline is real, but the exact August figures will improve somewhat.
